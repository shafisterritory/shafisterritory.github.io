02 Research · in progress

Towards Embodiment-Agnostic
Human-to-Robot Motion Retargeting

A position paper on open challenges — M.Sc. Autonomous Systems, Hochschule Bonn-Rhein-Sieg

Robots that learn by watching people could shed most of the expert programming that keeps them out of factories, hospitals and homes. The idea is simple: record what a person does, transfer it to a robot. The difficulty lies entirely in the transfer.

Human and robot bodies differ in joints, link lengths and degrees of freedom, so copying joint angles almost never produces valid robot motion. A decade of progress has narrowed this gap but narrowly. Methods get demonstrated on one robot: a Pepper, a NAO, a single dexterous hand. Moving to a new platform means redoing much of the engineering.

I argue that this per-robot specialisation, rather than any single technical bottleneck, is now the principal obstacle to scaling demonstration-based robot learning.

Geometric & kinematic mapping

Define an explicit correspondence between human and robot. Khalil et al. scale human limb vectors proportionally to a Pepper's links cheap, runs on ordinary video, but sensitive to the noise and depth ambiguity of single-camera pose estimation.

Trade-off Cheap and interpretable; brittle and low-precision.

Optimisation-based retargeting

Cast retargeting as a constrained problem: minimise human–robot pose discrepancy subject to joint limits and collision avoidance. DexPilot teleoperates a 23-DoA hand-arm system from four depth cameras watching a bare hand — accurate and general within a hand morphology.

Trade-off Precise; computationally heavy and cost terms tuned per robot.

Learning-based retargeting

Replace hand-designed rules with mappings learned from data. Zhang et al. search a compressed latent space for the best-matching robot pose, transferring two-armed sign language across YuMi, NAO and Pepper. The binding constraint is how much matched human–robot data you need.

Trade-off Scales across robots; starved for paired training data.

Physics-aware imitation

Enforce dynamic feasibility, not just kinematic similarity — the motion must be executable, not merely reachable. This family speaks directly to the feasibility gap, where a motion comfortable for a person violates a robot's torque limits or is dynamically impossible.

Trade-off Physically valid; expensive and simulator-dependent.

Three open problems cut across all four families

01

Feasibility gap

A motion comfortable for a person may violate joint limits, cause self-collision, or be dynamically impossible to execute.

02

Morphology dependence

Structural mismatch and degree-of-freedom mismatch mean the mapping is re-engineered for every new robot body.

03

Input-quality sensitivity

Pipelines degrade sharply as the motion source drops from optical mocap to a single monocular camera.

The position. The next advance must come from embodiment-agnostic retargeting: a pipeline that takes a robot's kinematic and dynamic description — a URDF — as runtime input rather than baking robot-specific parameters into training or design, produces physically feasible trajectories without per-robot cost tuning, and applies across structurally different classes: a serial arm, a humanoid torso, a dexterous hand, with no architectural change.

04 About

I like problems where the map
runs out.

I am a Master's student in Autonomous Systems at Hochschule Bonn-Rhein-Sieg, with three years of engineering behind me in autonomous mobile robotics, sensor fusion, and AI-based perception.

Most of my work has lived in the gap between a clean simulation and a messy room. A nav stack that behaves perfectly in Gazebo meets a corridor with glass walls and reflective floors; a face-recognition model that hits its benchmark drops frames on an Orin Nano at 15 watts. I find that gap the interesting part of the job, and I have spent most of my career closing it on service robots, semi-humanoids, an autonomous boat, and now a driverless racing car.

I am looking for roles where autonomy, perception and mobility meet real hardware. I am based in Bonn and open to relocating within Germany and the EU.
